As a Product Owner,

you decide what is worth building,

and whether it was built right.

These are where you are most involved in:

01 01
Discover / Context

Assemble requirements, design and brownfield context as input to the PDLC.

02 02
Specify

Turn approved requirements into a buildable specification with clear priorities and business decisions.

03 03
Plan

Validate that scope and delivery plan align with business priorities before work begins.

04 04
Tasks

Review task breakdowns and ensure acceptance criteria match your approved specification.

05 05
Implement

Stay available to unblock decisions as agents build to spec — scope creep still needs your veto.

06 06
Test & Validate

Confirm that what was built actually delivers the value stated in the PRD, not just that it works.

07 07
Build / Ship

Give deployment sign-off once you're satisfied that the right thing ships to the right users.

08 08
GreenLane CI/CD

Ensure the pipeline and backlog stay aligned with your approved priorities through each build cycle.

09 09
Operate & Learn

Own whether the expected value showed up. Use what the squad learns to feed the next PDLC cycle.

Stage 1. Discover/ Context

What you are responsible for

Agents can draft a BRD in minutes, but they cannot tell you whether the problem is worth solving. In this stage you make sure the requirements context that every later stage builds on reflects what the business actually needs.

BRD

Are the right stakeholders named, contradictions resolved, objectives quantified and scope boundaries clear? Approve it, or send it back with corrections.

PRD

Is every story tagged P0, P1 or P2, is the MVP boundary obvious, and are technical constraints and non functional requirements present?

Stage 2. Specify

What you are responsible for

You turn approved requirements into a buildable specification by owning the priorities and the answers to the business questions that come up.

Backlog

Do the epics and stories reflect your priorities, and does each story have Given/When/Then acceptance criteria and a parent epic? Confirm priority, scope and sequencing.

Clarifications

Which gaps and conflicts need a business answer?

spec.md

Does it trace back to your requirements, with open questions resolved?

Stage 9. Operate & Learn

What you are responsible for

Product squads own outcomes, not only delivery, so you stay responsible for whether the expected value actually showed up. Ongoing operational review sits with the Ops or System Manager, and your part is to use what they find.

Insights and user feedback

Did the value we expected show up?

Updates to context

Does what was learned need to feed back into the BRD, PRD or backlog so the next run starts from the truth?

These are the agents you'll most likely work with:

Research Planner

Turns your vision and stakeholder notes into an assumptions log, a research plan, interview guides and screeners.

BRD Builder

Converts business problems, objectives and discovery inputs into a structured and traceable Business Requirements Document.

PRD Builder

Translates approved business direction into detailed, measurable and testable product requirements for downstream design, engineering and validation.

Jira Backlog Manager

Transforms approved product requirements or epics into a backlog of epics, user stories and acceptance criteria.
