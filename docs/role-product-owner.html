Stage 01. Mobilise & Assess

What you are responsible for

You set the direction for the increment. The Research Planner needs to know what you're trying to achieve and why, so it can produce a research plan that's grounded in the right questions.

Product direction

What is the increment meant to achieve? What business outcome should it deliver? Be specific — the Research Planner uses this to frame the right research questions.

Scope and constraints

What is in and out of scope? What boundaries, known risks or dependencies should shape the work?

Stage 02. Discover & Frame

What you are responsible for

You confirm whether discovery is addressing the right problem. Agents can synthesise research quickly, but only you can say whether the findings match the problem you set out to solve and whether the priorities are still right.

Research plan

Does the plan target the right users and the right questions? Are the research objectives clearly connected to the product outcome you've set?

Insights synthesis

Do the synthesised themes and recommendations reflect a real user or business problem? Are the findings relevant to your product priorities?

Stage 04. Define requirements

What you are responsible for

You confirm the product intent and resolve scope and priority decisions. The agents draft requirements from approved inputs, but only you can confirm that the business case, priorities and scope are correct.

BRD

Do the objectives and requirements accurately reflect the business problem? Are stakeholders named, scope clear and success criteria measurable?

PRD

Does every requirement trace back to the BRD? Are priorities set, the MVP scope clear and non-functional requirements included?

Stage 05. Design experience & architecture

What you are responsible for

You review the proposed experience to confirm it matches what you've approved. The Experience Architect translates requirements into design, but you check that the product direction hasn't been lost in translation.

Experience design

Does the proposed experience reflect the approved product direction and user outcomes? Are the flows, entry points and key interactions right?

Stage 06. Create backlog & specs

What you are responsible for

You own the backlog. You confirm that requirements have been correctly broken down into stories, that priorities are reflected and that the acceptance criteria match what you agreed.

Jira backlog

Are epics and stories correctly structured? Do acceptance criteria reflect what you approved? Are priority and MVP scope correctly represented?

Technical specification

Does the spec reflect the product intent? Have scope and priority decisions been carried through correctly from the PRD?

Stage 07. Plan and decompose

What you are responsible for

You review the implementation plan to confirm that scope and priority trade-offs are acceptable. Where dependencies affect the value being delivered, you decide whether to adjust scope or accept the sequencing.

Implementation plan

Does the sequencing deliver the highest-priority value first? Are any scope trade-offs or dependencies acceptable to the business?

Stage 09. Validate

What you are responsible for

You confirm whether the increment delivers what was agreed. Test agents run the checks, but you review the results and decide whether the product meets the acceptance criteria you set.

API test results

Do API failures indicate a gap in the product, or an issue with the test or specification?

Functional test results

Does the increment meet the acceptance criteria? Are any test failures blocking release?

Stage 10. Release

What you are responsible for

You give product acceptance before the increment is released. You confirm the agreed outcomes have been met and the business is ready to receive the change.

Functional test sign-off

Has all functional testing passed? Are any outstanding failures acceptable for this release?

Deployment confirmation

Is the deployment configuration consistent with what was approved? Are there any operational risks to flag before release?

Stage 11. Operate & Improve

What you are responsible for

You use what you learn from customers and operations to decide what to work on next. The Insights Synthesizer can aggregate feedback and usage signals; you use it to sharpen product priorities for the next increment.

Insights synthesis

What are customers telling you? Do the synthesised themes suggest a change in product direction, priorities or acceptance criteria for the next increment?

These are the agents you'll most likely work with:

A.1

Research Planner

Turns your vision and stakeholder notes into an assumptions log, a research plan, interview guides and screeners.
A.2

Insights Synthesizer

Synthesises raw research data into structured findings: themes, personas, journey maps and design context packages.
A.3

Experience Architect

Translates approved requirements, designs and standards into a complete UX specification covering flows, components, interactions and frontend behaviour.
A.4

Context Builder

Creates a unified project context from business, technical, compliance, standards, documentation and codebase.
A.5

BRD Builder

Converts business problems, objectives and discovery inputs into a structured and traceable Business Requirements Document.
A.6

PRD Builder

Translates approved business direction into detailed, measurable and testable product requirements for design, engineering and validation.
A.7

Jira Backlog Manager

Transforms approved product requirements or epics into a backlog of epics, user stories and acceptance criteria.
A.8

Technical Specifier

Converts approved requirements, architecture, design and standards into an implementation-ready specification while identifying gaps.
A.9

Implementation Planner

Decomposes the approved specification and solution approach into ordered implementation tasks with explicit dependencies and expected outcomes.
A.11

API Testing

Generates and executes API tests against approved interface specifications and acceptance criteria, producing test results and evidence for review.
A.12

Functional Testing

Converts approved requirements, designs and acceptance criteria into repeatable functional tests, executable scripts and test evidence.
