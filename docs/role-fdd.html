As a Forward Deployed Designer,

you make sure what gets built is something people

can understand, trust and actually use.

These are where you are most involved in:

01 01
Discover / Context

Assemble requirements, design and brownfield context as input to the PDLC.

02 02
Specify

Turn approved requirements into a buildable specification with clear priorities and business decisions.

03 03
Plan

Validate that scope and delivery plan align with business priorities before work begins.

04 04
Tasks

Review task breakdowns and ensure acceptance criteria match your approved specification.

05 05
Implement

Stay available to unblock decisions as agents build to spec — scope creep still needs your veto.

06 06
Test & Validate

Confirm that what was built actually delivers the value stated in the PRD, not just that it works.

07 07
Build / Ship

Give deployment sign-off once you’re satisfied that the right thing ships to the right users.

08 08
GreenLane CI/CD

Ensure the pipeline and backlog stay aligned with your approved priorities through each build cycle.

09 09
Operate & Learn

Own whether the expected value showed up. Use what the squad learns to feed the next PDLC cycle.

Stage 1. Discover / Context

What you are responsible for

Product sees one problem, engineering builds another, and the gap shows up later as rework. You close that gap early by understanding the real problem before anyone automates it. In this stage you turn user needs into the design context that every later agent will work from, which covers journeys, design system standards, UI patterns and components.

Research plan

Are the assumptions, questions and screeners the right ones before any fieldwork starts? Sign it off, or revise it.

Conducting discovery

Are your assumptions of user needs, pain points, and assumptions validated?

Insights, personas, and blueprints

Is each finding traceable to its source evidence, and do the conclusions hold up? Accept them, or challenge them — deciding what the findings mean is still a human call.

Design context package

Are the journeys, design system and UI patterns current and authoritative?

Stage 2. Specify

What you are responsible for

You make sure the specification carries the design intent, not just the functional requirements. Frontend behaviour that is not written down here is behaviour the agent will invent, so your job is to make the design evidence visible and complete.

Detailed designs

Does the design cover every user story while maintaining best usability principles?

Design evidence in spec.md

Is it identified for every piece of frontend work, with any pending manual uploads clearly flagged?

UI behaviour and states

Are interactions, error states and recovery paths specified, and not left to interpretation?

Stage 5. Implement

What you are responsible for

You are the person who can tell whether what was built is what was designed. The code review gate belongs to the FDE, but design fidelity is a named review point, and you are best placed to judge it.

Frontend screens

Do layout, components, states and copy match the design, and has the agent substituted its own idea of what looks right?

These are the agents you'll most likely work with:

Research Planner

Generates a structured research plan — objectives, screener criteria, question sets, and session guides — from a discovery brief and product context.

Insights Synthesizer

Synthesises raw research data into structured findings: themes, personas, journey maps and design context packages.

Experience Architect

Translates approved requirements, designs and standards into a complete UX specification covering flows, components, interactions and frontend behaviour.
