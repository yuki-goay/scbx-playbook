Proposal Keeping the evidence trail

New evidence is coming in. Decisions are being made. Work is piling up. The instinct is to keep moving and record it later. By the time "later" arrives, the reasons for a decision have faded, a new team member has made a different assumption, and a stakeholder is asking why a direction was chosen that nobody can fully explain. This capability is the practice of recording as you go, so the work can be trusted by anyone who inherits it.

Key artefacts

Activity UX artefact When it is worth doing
Log each decision as it is made: what was decided, why, who decided, what alternatives were considered and whether a human gate applied Decision log Always — every decision that could be questioned later deserves a record
Update the evidence repository when new evidence arrives or when a finding changes Evidence repository Every time new evidence comes in — not at the end of fieldwork
Flag gaps and contradictions in the evidence when they appear, so they are addressed rather than carried forward Gaps and contradictions list Continuously — the sooner a gap is named, the sooner the team can decide what to do with it
Prepare a stakeholder share-out: what has been found, what it means, what decisions were made and why Stakeholder share-out Regularly throughout the work — share before the findings are polished, not only at the end
Get sign-off at defined points — not at the end of every session, but at the points where the work changes direction Sign-off record At phase gates and major direction changes

What can agents do?

AG-01 Evidence Librarian logs decisions in the decision log with source links and rationale, updates the evidence repository with new items and assigns confidence levels, flags gaps and contradictions, and logs sign-off records.

Neither agent decides what the evidence means, invents entries, or promotes a finding from evidence-thin to evidence-grounded without a person's confirmation.

💡 Tips

Log decisions in real time, not at the end of the week. By then, the reasons have usually faded to "it seemed right at the time."

Share work before it is polished. A stakeholder who sees rough evidence early is much less likely to reject a clean recommendation late.

Gaps in the evidence are not failures. Name them so the team can decide whether to fill them or accept the uncertainty.

If the evidence contradicts the brief, that is a finding. Log it and raise it. Do not quietly resolve the contradiction by choosing the brief.

The decision log is for the next reader, not just you. Write each entry as if the person reading it has no prior knowledge of the project.

Where to go next

  • Applies to: the entirety of discovery, definition and design. Return whenever new evidence arrives, a decision is made, or a stakeholder needs to see the work.
  • Feeds: every capability that produces an artefact depends on the evidence trail to stay trustworthy. Handoff puts the decision log into every handoff package.
