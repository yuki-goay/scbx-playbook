# Structure the experience

You have a full picture of your users and how their journey could go. What you do not have yet is a shape: where things live, what they are called, how people move between them, and what rules you will hold every design to. This capability gives you that shape.

---

## Key artefacts

| Activity                                                                                                                               | UX artefact                                 | When it is worth doing                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| List every piece of content and every function the experience needs, with its source and owner. Mark what to keep, change or drop      | Content and function inventory              | You are replacing or reshaping something that already exists, or content owners are unclear        |
| Choose the navigation model, write labels in the user's own words and set the rules for search and wayfinding                          | Navigation model and label set              | The structure has more than a handful of areas, or users have to move between them to finish a job |
| List each screen and what it holds, draw how screens connect for each key journey, then tie every screen to a story, job or pain point | Sitemap, screen inventory and flow diagrams | You have a navigation model and need a plan of what to design                                      |
| Test whether people can find things. Run a tree test or closed card sort on the key tasks, record where people go wrong and revise     | Findability results and revised structure   | The structure is new or changed, and finding things is a main job                                  |
| Check the structure against the experience principles and constraints, then agree it with stakeholders                                 | Information architecture (final)            | Always, before the structure goes into detailed design                                             |

---

## What can agents do?

**AG-02 Research Synthesis** collects the candidate qualities for the experience principles from the inputs and removes duplicates, citing a source for each.

**AG-04 Design Organiser** lists content and functions, drafts the sitemap, screen inventory and flows, and traces each screen to a story or job.

**AG-03 Research Ops** prepares the findability test and sends it once approved.

**AG-01 Evidence Librarian** records where people go wrong in the findability test.

---

> 💡 **Tips**
>
> **Start from the to-be blueprint, not the old system.** To reimagine the experience, we must move on from how people do work. Copying the existing structure carries its problems into the new one. You can trust your new process proposal knowing that you have done extensive research on how people work and where friction lies in the existing system.
